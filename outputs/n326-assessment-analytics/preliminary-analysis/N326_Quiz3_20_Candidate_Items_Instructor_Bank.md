## Suicide screening and safety

### Item 1 — Direct assessment after a suicide cue

**Format:** Single-best-answer  
**Tags:** Suicide screening; Analyze cues; Assessment; Psychosocial integrity  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

A client hospitalized with major depressive disorder tells the nurse, “My family would be better off without me.” Which response should the nurse make **first**?

A. “Your family would be very upset if anything happened to you.”  
B. “Are you thinking about killing yourself right now?”  
C. “Tell me what happened with your family this week.”  
D. “Please sign an agreement that you will not hurt yourself.”

**Key:** B

**Rationale:** The statement is a direct suicide cue. The nurse should assess current suicidal thoughts clearly and without euphemism before exploring other stressors. Reassurance and a no-harm agreement do not replace risk assessment. [S1, S2]

**Distractor notes:** C is a therapeutic question but does not first establish immediate safety. A minimizes the cue. D substitutes a contract for assessment.

### Item 2 — Risk and protective cues

**Format:** Matrix  
**Tags:** Suicide risk; Recognize cues; Clinical judgment; Safety  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

A 22-year-old client is evaluated after telling a friend, “I cannot keep doing this.” For each finding, identify whether it is primarily a **risk cue** or a **protective cue** in the current assessment.

| Finding | Risk cue | Protective cue |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Suicide attempt 8 months ago | ✓ |  |
| Current access to an unsecured firearm | ✓ |  |
| Recent end of an important relationship | ✓ |  |
| Supportive roommate who is available tonight |  | ✓ |
| States, “Finishing nursing school still matters to me” |  | ✓ |
| Willing to contact a trusted support person during a crisis |  | ✓ |

**Key/rationale:** Prior suicidal behavior, accessible lethal means, and a recent major loss increase concern. Social connection, articulated reasons for living, and willingness to seek help can be protective, but no protective factor independently rules out suicide risk. [S1, S2]

### Item 3 — Cues requiring immediate escalation

**Format:** Select N — select 2  
**Tags:** Suicide acuity; Prioritize hypotheses; Assessment; Safety  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

Which **two** findings most strongly indicate that the client requires immediate safety intervention?

A. The client began psychotherapy last week.  
B. The client is thinking about suicide now.  
C. The client reports sleeping four hours per night.  
D. The client has a specific plan and immediate access to the intended method.  
E. The client’s sibling has offered transportation to appointments.

**Key:** B and D

**Rationale:** Current suicidal thoughts establish acute concern; a specific plan with accessible means further increases immediacy. Sleep disruption can contribute to risk but is not as urgent as current ideation with plan and access. [S1, S2]

### Item 4 — Immediate inpatient safety actions

**Format:** Select all that apply  
**Tags:** Suicide precautions; Take action; Implementation; Safety  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

A hospitalized client reports current suicidal thoughts and describes a plan. Which actions should the nurse take immediately? **Select all that apply.**

A. Maintain observation consistent with the client’s current acuity and agency policy.  
B. Remove or secure environmental objects that could be used for self-harm.  
C. Ask the client to sign a no-suicide contract before continuing the assessment.  
D. Notify the responsible clinician and initiate an urgent safety evaluation.  
E. Leave the client alone in a quiet room to reduce stimulation.  
F. Promise that the client’s disclosure will remain confidential.

**Key:** A, B, and D

**Rationale:** Current ideation with a plan requires observation, environmental safety, and urgent clinical evaluation. The client should not be left alone. Safety information cannot be promised absolute confidentiality, and a contract does not substitute for assessment and safety planning. [S1, S3]

### Item 5 — Collaborative safety planning

**Format:** Single-best-answer  
**Tags:** Safety planning; Generate solutions; Teaching; Psychosocial integrity  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

Which client statement best demonstrates an effective collaborative safety plan?

A. “I signed a form promising that I will not attempt suicide.”  
B. “If I feel worse, I will wait until my next appointment before telling anyone.”  
C. “I identified my warning signs, coping steps, people I can contact, crisis resources, and how lethal means will be secured.”  
D. “My partner is responsible for deciding whether my suicidal thoughts are serious.”

**Key:** C

**Rationale:** A practical safety plan identifies warning signs, internal coping strategies, supportive contacts, professional/crisis resources, and steps to reduce access to lethal means. It is collaborative and specific rather than a promise or transfer of responsibility. [S2, S3]

## Bipolar disorder and mania

### Item 6 — Antidepressant monotherapy in bipolar I disorder

**Format:** Cloze/dropdown  
**Tags:** Bipolar depression; Medication safety; Analyze cues; Pharmacological therapies  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

A client with bipolar I disorder has a new prescription for sertraline and no mood stabilizer or antipsychotic on the medication list.

Complete the sentence:

The nurse should **[clarify the prescription / administer the first dose immediately / teach the client to stop the drug when mood improves]** because antidepressant monotherapy may **[trigger mania or rapid cycling / cause lithium toxicity / prevent future mood episodes]**.

**Key:** Clarify the prescription; trigger mania or rapid cycling.

**Rationale:** In bipolar disorder, antidepressants may be added to a mood stabilizer or atypical antipsychotic, but antidepressants are generally not used alone because of the risk of a manic episode or rapid cycling. [S4]

### Item 7 — Priority in acute mania

**Format:** Single-best-answer  
**Tags:** Acute mania; Prioritize hypotheses; Safety; Management of care  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

A client experiencing acute mania has not slept or eaten for three days and was brought to the hospital after walking into traffic while directing cars. Which goal has the highest priority during the initial phase of care?

A. The client will identify two triggers for the manic episode.  
B. The client will remain safe and accept immediate physiologic support.  
C. The client will participate in a one-hour psychotherapy group.  
D. The client will develop a six-month relapse-prevention plan.

**Key:** B

**Rationale:** Dangerous impulsivity plus sleep and nutritional deprivation makes immediate safety and physiologic stabilization the priority. Insight-oriented work and long-term planning occur after acute symptoms improve. [S4]

### Item 8 — Nursing care during acute mania

**Format:** Select N — select 3  
**Tags:** Mania; Generate solutions; Implementation; Basic care and comfort  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

Which **three** interventions should the nurse include for a client with acute mania?

A. Give brief, concrete directions.  
B. Offer portable high-calorie foods and fluids.  
C. Schedule rest periods in a lower-stimulation setting.  
D. Encourage competitive group activities to use excess energy.  
E. Explore the meaning of grandiose beliefs through lengthy discussion.  
F. Offer caffeinated beverages when the client reports fatigue.

**Key:** A, B, and C

**Rationale:** Acute mania reduces attention, sleep, judgment, and the ability to remain seated for meals. Concise communication, accessible nutrition, reduced stimulation, and rest address immediate needs. Competition, prolonged discussion, and caffeine can worsen stimulation.

### Item 9 — Limit-setting without escalation

**Format:** Single-best-answer  
**Tags:** Mania; Therapeutic communication; Take action; Psychosocial integrity  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

A client with acute mania repeatedly enters other clients’ rooms and takes their belongings. The client becomes irritable when redirected. Which response is best?

A. “The other clients are upset, so you need to apologize to the group now.”  
B. “Do not enter other clients’ rooms. Walk with me to the dayroom, where you may choose one of these two activities.”  
C. “Why do you think you have difficulty respecting other people’s boundaries?”  
D. “You have lost all group privileges for the next 24 hours.”

**Key:** B

**Rationale:** The response states a clear behavioral limit, uses concise language, and immediately redirects the client to a structured alternative. Public confrontation, exploratory questioning during acute mania, and broad punitive restrictions can increase conflict.

### Item 10 — Lithium toxicity after sodium and fluid loss

**Format:** Single-best-answer  
**Tags:** Lithium; Recognize cues; Medication safety; Reduction of risk  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

A client taking lithium reports three days of diarrhea after beginning a low-sodium diet. The nurse observes a coarse tremor and unsteady gait. Which action should the nurse take first?

A. Administer the scheduled lithium with a full glass of water.  
B. Hold the lithium and notify the prescriber for urgent evaluation and a serum level.  
C. Encourage the client to continue restricting sodium until the diarrhea resolves.  
D. Recommend an over-the-counter antidiarrheal and reassess the client tomorrow.

**Key:** B

**Rationale:** Diarrhea, sodium depletion, coarse tremor, and gait disturbance are concerning for lithium toxicity. The medication should be withheld pending urgent evaluation rather than administered or managed only as a gastrointestinal complaint. [S5]

### Item 11 — Lithium discharge teaching

**Format:** Select all that apply  
**Tags:** Lithium; Patient education; Implementation; Pharmacological therapies  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

Which instructions should the nurse include for a client taking lithium? **Select all that apply.**

A. Maintain a consistent, normal dietary sodium intake.  
B. Maintain adequate hydration, especially during heat or exercise.  
C. Contact the prescriber for persistent vomiting, diarrhea, fever, or heavy sweating.  
D. Check with the prescriber or pharmacist before starting an NSAID or diuretic.  
E. Keep appointments for serum lithium, kidney, and thyroid monitoring.  
F. Begin a salt-free diet to reduce fluid retention.

**Key:** A, B, C, D, and E

**Rationale:** Changes in sodium balance, dehydration, illness, NSAIDs, and diuretics can increase lithium concentrations or toxicity risk. Ongoing serum, renal, and thyroid monitoring is required. A salt-free diet is unsafe unless specifically managed by the prescribing clinician. [S5]

### Item 12 — Lamotrigine and new rash

**Format:** Single-best-answer  
**Tags:** Lamotrigine; Recognize cues; Medication safety; Reduction of risk  
**Intended difficulty:** Foundational safety item

A client with bipolar I disorder began lamotrigine three weeks ago and now reports a new widespread rash. Which action should the nurse take?

A. Reassure the client that rash is expected during titration.  
B. Instruct the client to take the next dose with food.  
C. Hold the medication and arrange immediate prescriber evaluation.  
D. Recommend a topical antihistamine and reassess in one week.

**Key:** C

**Rationale:** Lamotrigine can cause serious skin reactions. Current labeling directs discontinuation at the first sign of rash unless the rash is clearly unrelated, so prompt evaluation is required. [S6]

### Item 13 — Lithium findings: monitor or escalate

**Format:** Matrix  
**Tags:** Lithium; Analyze cues; Evaluation; Pharmacological therapies  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate–challenging

For each finding in a client taking lithium, identify whether it is generally appropriate to **monitor and reinforce teaching** or whether it **requires prompt clinical follow-up**.

| Finding | Monitor/reinforce teaching | Prompt clinical follow-up |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Mild fine hand tremor shortly after treatment begins | ✓ |  |
| Mild thirst with stable laboratory results | ✓ |  |
| Coarse tremor with new ataxia |  | ✓ |
| Persistent vomiting and diarrhea |  | ✓ |
| Client began taking ibuprofen every day without discussing it with the prescriber |  | ✓ |
| New confusion and slurred speech |  | ✓ |

**Key/rationale:** Mild thirst and a fine tremor can occur during treatment, but neurologic changes, significant gastrointestinal fluid loss, and an unreviewed NSAID require follow-up because they can signal or precipitate toxicity. [S5]

## Depression and mood disorders

### Item 14 — Early antidepressant response

**Format:** Single-best-answer  
**Tags:** SSRI treatment; Evaluate outcomes; Patient education; Pharmacological therapies  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

After two weeks of sertraline, a client reports improved sleep and appetite but continues to feel depressed and hopeless. Which interpretation and action are most appropriate?

A. The medication has failed and should be stopped immediately.  
B. Early vegetative improvement can precede mood improvement; continue close monitoring, including direct suicide assessment.  
C. Improved sleep indicates that the client is developing mania.  
D. Suicide-risk monitoring is no longer necessary because some symptoms improved.

**Key:** B

**Rationale:** Antidepressants often require several weeks for full benefit, and sleep, appetite, energy, or concentration can improve before mood. Persistent hopelessness still requires direct safety assessment and close monitoring. [S7]

### Item 15 — Serotonin syndrome

**Format:** Bow-tie  
**Tags:** Serotonin syndrome; Recognize cues; Take action; Physiological adaptation  
**Intended difficulty:** Challenging

A client taking sertraline began sumatriptan one week ago. The client presents with agitation, diarrhea, temperature 38.9°C (102.0°F), heart rate 116/min, hyperreflexia, and inducible ankle clonus.

**Select the most likely condition:**

- Serotonin syndrome  
- Neuroleptic malignant syndrome  
- Anticholinergic toxicity  
- Lithium toxicity

**Select two actions:**

- Withhold serotonergic medications pending urgent evaluation  
- Initiate urgent provider/emergency response and supportive monitoring  
- Administer the next sertraline dose to prevent withdrawal  
- Encourage the client to ambulate to decrease muscle stiffness  
- Delay intervention until a routine follow-up appointment

**Select two parameters to monitor most closely:**

- Temperature and autonomic instability  
- Clonus and hyperreflexia  
- Bowel sounds and urinary retention only  
- Serum thyroid-stimulating hormone  
- Visual acuity

**Key:** Serotonin syndrome; withhold serotonergic medications; initiate urgent response/supportive monitoring; temperature/autonomic instability; clonus/hyperreflexia.

**Rationale:** The serotonergic combination plus agitation, gastrointestinal symptoms, hyperthermia, hyperreflexia, and clonus is characteristic of serotonin syndrome and requires urgent management. [S8]

### Item 16 — Hopelessness requires a safety assessment

**Format:** Single-best-answer  
**Tags:** Depression; Suicide cue; Analyze cues; Assessment  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

A client with major depressive disorder says, “Nothing will ever change. There is no point in trying anymore.” Which response should the nurse make first?

A. “Tell me which part of the day feels most difficult.”  
B. “Are you thinking about killing yourself or wishing you were dead?”  
C. “Your medication may need more time to work.”  
D. “Let us list the people who care about you.”

**Key:** B

**Rationale:** Hopelessness and the statement that there is “no point” are suicide cues. The nurse should establish safety directly before moving to broader exploration, education, or support planning. [S1, S7]

### Item 17 — Immediate outcomes for severe depression

**Format:** Select N — select 2  
**Tags:** Severe depression; Prioritize hypotheses; Planning; Basic care and comfort  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

A client with severe depression has lost 7 kg (15.4 lb), remains in bed most of the day, and reports intermittent urges to die. Which **two** short-term outcomes have the highest priority?

A. The client will consume an agreed minimum amount of food and fluids.  
B. The client will tell staff when suicidal urges increase.  
C. The client will attend three group sessions each day.  
D. The client will identify a long-term career goal.  
E. The client will independently complete every activity of daily living.

**Key:** A and B

**Rationale:** Physiologic support and immediate suicide safety take priority. Group attendance, long-term planning, and full independence may be appropriate later but are not the initial priorities.

### Item 18 — Grief versus a depressive disorder

**Format:** Single-best-answer  
**Tags:** Grief; Depression differential; Analyze cues; Psychosocial integrity  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

Six months after the death of a parent, a client reports crying when hearing the parent’s favorite song and feeling waves of sadness near holidays. The client has returned to work, maintains relationships, can experience positive emotions, and denies worthlessness or suicidal thoughts. Which interpretation is most appropriate?

A. The findings establish prolonged grief disorder.  
B. The findings demonstrate treatment-resistant major depression.  
C. The pattern can be consistent with grief; continue assessing distress, functioning, culture, and safety.  
D. The client should avoid reminders of the parent until sadness resolves.

**Key:** C

**Rationale:** Trigger-linked waves of sadness with preserved function and capacity for positive emotion can occur in grief. Prolonged grief disorder in adults requires persistent, impairing symptoms beyond 12 months and consideration of cultural context. [S9]

### Item 19 — Depression treatment response

**Format:** Matrix  
**Tags:** Depression; Evaluate outcomes; Clinical judgment; Psychosocial integrity  
**Intended difficulty:** Moderate

For each finding, identify whether it **supports improvement** or **requires priority follow-up**.

| Finding | Supports improvement | Priority follow-up |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Initiates conversation and resumes a previously valued activity | ✓ |  |
| Sleeps 6–7 hours and eats most meals | ✓ |  |
| Reports a current suicide plan and access to the intended method |  | ✓ |
| Stops the antidepressant abruptly because “I feel better now” |  | ✓ |
| Develops markedly decreased need for sleep, rapid speech, and impulsive spending |  | ✓ |

**Key/rationale:** Functional engagement, sleep, and nutrition support improvement. Active suicide risk, unplanned medication discontinuation, and emerging manic symptoms require follow-up. Antidepressant monotherapy can precipitate mania in a person with bipolar disorder. [S4, S7]

### Item 20 — Tricyclic antidepressant safety

**Format:** Single-best-answer  
**Tags:** Tricyclic antidepressants; Recognize cues; Medication safety; Reduction of risk  
**Intended difficulty:** Foundational safety item

A client taking amitriptyline reports several new findings. Which finding should the nurse report to the prescriber immediately?

A. Mild dry mouth  
B. Mild constipation  
C. Drowsiness at bedtime  
D. Syncope accompanied by palpitations

**Key:** D

**Rationale:** Syncope with palpitations can indicate a clinically important cardiovascular adverse effect. Amitriptyline labeling includes conduction changes, arrhythmias, orthostatic hypotension, syncope, tachycardia, and palpitations. [S10]

## Suggested 15-item selection

One balanced starting form from this bank would be Items 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 12, 14, 15, 17, 18, 19, and 20.

This selection gives:

- Suicide/safety: 4 items.
- Bipolar/mania: 5 items.
- Depression/mood: 6 items.
- Single-best-answer: 8 items.
- Matrix: 2 items.
- Multiple-response/Select N: 3 items.
- Cloze: 1 item.
- Bow-tie: 1 item.

Items 1–5 can also be adapted into a shorter linked suicide case. If this is done, avoid scoring essentially the same cue twice; each item should measure a different judgment step.

### Optional data-informed substitution — lithium calculation

If medication calculation belongs in the Quiz 3 blueprint, consider substituting this novel transfer item for one of the 20 candidates rather than reusing the Exam 1 calculation:

A client has a prescription for lithium 450 mg PO every 12 hours. The available liquid preparation is labeled 300 mg per 5 mL. How many mL should the nurse administer per dose? Record the answer to the nearest tenth if needed.

**Key:** 7.5 mL

**Calculation:** 450 mg × (5 mL / 300 mg) = 7.5 mL.

This is intentionally a different dose from the Exam 1 item so it checks transfer rather than recall of the previous answer.

## Post-administration review plan

- Target most items for local facility between .60 and .90.
- Treat facility ≥.95 as a purposeful foundational check only; otherwise revise or replace.
- Review local corrected item-rest correlations below .10, but do not retire an item solely on one cohort of approximately 10 students.
- Inspect response patterns for nonfunctioning distractors and keyed-response ambiguity.
- Preserve a versioned item ID and tags for content, nursing process/NCJMM step, client need, format, and intended difficulty.
- Record any faculty edits before administration so the revised item is not pooled with a materially different earlier version.
