# N326 preliminary analysis and Quiz 3 recommendations

Analysis date: 2026-07-31  
Data included: 24 assessments, 331 student attempts, 663 item records, and 9,175 item-response records from January–July 2026. Student-level calculations were performed with pseudonymous keys; no student names, IDs, or emails are included in the outputs.

## Executive interpretation

The broad course averages are reasonably stable across the three completed sessions, but the current Quiz 3 is not functioning well as an Exam 2 readiness check.

- Mean Quiz 3 scores were 85.8% in January, 85.3% in March, and 94.5% in May.
- Among 43 students with both scores, Quiz 3 and Exam 2 correlated only weakly: Pearson *r* = .13 and Spearman rho = .22. After adjusting for session, the Quiz 3 slope was not statistically distinguishable from zero (*p* = .296; adjusted R² = .03).
- Seven students scored below 75% on Exam 2. All seven had passed Quiz 3. Thus, using Quiz 3 <75% as an early-warning rule had 0% sensitivity in these cohorts.
- Seventeen of the 45 administered Quiz 3 items had local facility of at least 95%; 17 produced no score variance at all. Twenty additional items had a negative or very-low local corrected item-rest correlation (<.10).
- May was the clearest ceiling case: 9 of 15 items were answered perfectly, the total-score standard deviation was only 0.52 points, and 13 of 15 stems were exact reuses from earlier sessions.
- Exploratory internal-consistency estimates were poor: raw Cronbach alpha was -.27, .38, and -3.18 for January, March, and May. With cohorts of 10–19, multidimensional content, partial-credit items, and severe ceiling effects, alpha is unstable and should not be treated as a precise population estimate. The practical conclusion is narrower: the total Quiz 3 score is not currently a dependable way to rank students or identify who needs Exam 2 support.

## Broader patterns and trends

### Cohort means

| Session | Mean quiz score | Mean exam score | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Jan 2026 | 82.1% | 81.8% | Baseline completed session |
| Mar 2026 | 82.3% | 83.9% | Slightly stronger exam performance |
| May 2026 | 83.9% | 83.7% | Similar to March |
| Jul 2026 | 79.2% | 79.3% | Preliminary: only Quiz 1, Quiz 2, and Exam 1 are present |

The July decline should not yet be interpreted as a cohort effect because the session is incomplete and contains only 29 attempts.

### Later assessments are consistently harder

Within the same students, Quiz 5 averaged 7.4 percentage points below Quiz 1 in January, 7.3 points below in March, and 2.9 points below in May. Exam 2 averaged 5.1, 1.8, and 4.2 points below Exam 1 in those sessions. This is more consistent with later/cumulative content or form difficulty than with proof that students “regressed.” It argues for equating the cognitive demand and content mix across assessments before interpreting score changes as progression.

### Other assessments may provide better—but still modest—warning signals

Exploratory pooled correlations with Exam 2 were:

| Prior assessment | Pearson *r* with Exam 2 | Session-adjusted model, adjusted R² |
|---|---:|---:|
| Quiz 1 | .16 | .05 |
| Quiz 2 | .33 | .13 |
| Quiz 3 | .13 | .03 |
| Quiz 4 | .42 | .12 |
| Quiz 5 | .22 | .10 |
| Exam 1 | .34 | .12 |

Quiz 4 was the largest simple correlate, while Quiz 2 and Exam 1 also outperformed Quiz 3. None of the individual slopes remained below .05 after correcting the six exploratory comparisons, so these are signals to monitor rather than validated predictors.

### The vendor/PDF statistics and local performance are not interchangeable

For Quiz 3, the correlation between the PDF point-biserial and the locally calculated corrected item-rest correlation was only .26. Identical reused items often carried identical PDF statistics while their local facility varied substantially. This suggests the PDF statistics may reflect a persistent or pooled item-bank history rather than only the current local cohort. Use both, but prioritize local facility, local item-rest correlation, distractor behavior, and qualitative review when making a current-form decision.

### Reuse statistics show substantial cohort sensitivity

There were 32 unique normalized stems among 45 Quiz 3 item administrations. Thirteen exact-stem groups were reused. Examples of local facility changes across March and May include:

- Bipolar medication/rash item: 63.2% to 90.0%.
- Sudden bright affect after prior suicide attempts: 73.7% to 100%.
- Acute-mania agitation/de-escalation item: 68.4% to 90.0%.
- Serotonin-syndrome item: 84.2% to 100%.

With these sample sizes, a single administration is not enough to label an item intrinsically “easy,” “hard,” or “bad.” At the same time, using 13 exact repeats on a 15-item form weakens form security and reduces independent evidence about the breadth of student understanding.

## Quiz 3 alignment with Exam 2

### Content distribution

Across the three Exam 2 forms, the structured topic tags identified 39 items in the intended Quiz 3 content domain: 13 per form. Their approximate distribution was:

- Bipolar/mania: 4–5 items per Exam 2.
- Depression: 4 items in January and March, 7 in May.
- Suicide/safety: 2–4 items.
- One adjacent PTSD/trauma-care item in January and March used a shared suicide-risk case.

Recent Quiz 3 forms instead emphasized suicide and familiar antidepressant teaching while leaving several recurring Exam 2 targets absent. Across the three Exam 2 forms there were four ECT items, four lithium items, and one tricyclic-antidepressant safety item. None of those targets appeared clearly on Quiz 3; the bipolar rash item assessed a different medication-safety decision.

### Response format and clinical judgment

Twelve of the 39 relevant Exam 2 items used a nontraditional response format: matrix, multiple response, cloze/dropdown, or Select N. Quiz 3 had eight multiple-response items and one hot spot across 45 administrations, but no matrix or cloze/dropdown items. A short linked case would better prepare students for transferring knowledge across a record, recognizing cues, prioritizing hypotheses, and selecting actions. The 2026 NCLEX-RN test plan explicitly measures clinical judgment through case-study and stand-alone items: <https://www.ncsbn.org/publications/2026-nclex-rn-test-plan>.

## Recommended 15-item blueprint

| Domain | Items | Suggested emphasis |
|---|---:|---|
| Suicide screening and safety | 4 | Direct screening/acuity; dynamic and protective cues; means safety; disposition and observation |
| Depression and mood disorders | 6 | MDD assessment and priorities; grief differential; treatment response; ECT; antidepressant monitoring |
| Bipolar disorder and mania | 5 | Recognition; milieu/nutrition/sleep; limit-setting/de-escalation; lithium; lamotrigine/rash safety |
| **Total** | **15** | Medication and clinical-judgment items are embedded within their disorder domains |

Suggested format mix within those 15 items:

- 8 single-best-answer items.
- 3 multiple-response or Select N items.
- 2 matrix items.
- 2 cloze/dropdown or other NGN-style items.
- Use three of the items as one linked mini-case rather than 15 unrelated vignettes.

Suggested intended facility mix after piloting:

- 3 foundational items around .80–.90.
- 9 application items around .65–.85.
- 3 complex clinical-judgment items around .50–.70.

Avoid intentionally targeting facility above .90 except for a clearly identified safety “must know.”

## Six draft items for the next Quiz 3

These are starting points for faculty review, not automatically validated items. They intentionally target gaps and use different scenarios from Exam 2.

### 1. Direct suicide-risk assessment

A client hospitalized with major depressive disorder tells the nurse, “My family would be better off without me.” Which response should the nurse make **first**?

A. “Your family would be very upset if anything happened to you.”  
B. “Are you thinking about killing yourself right now?”  
C. “Tell me what happened with your family this week.”  
D. “Please sign this agreement that you will not hurt yourself.”

**Key: B.** A direct acuity question follows a positive suicide-risk cue. Reassurance and a no-harm agreement do not replace assessment. The NIMH ASQ pathway directs immediate assessment of current suicidal thoughts after a positive screen: <https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-conducted-at-nimh/asq-toolkit-materials>.

### 2. Linked suicide mini-case: Select 2

The client answers “yes” and states, “I plan to use the handgun in my nightstand when I go home tonight.” The client’s partner is present and willing to help. Which **two** findings require the most immediate escalation?

A. Current suicidal thoughts  
B. A supportive partner  
C. A specific plan with accessible lethal means  
D. Willingness to talk with the nurse  
E. No prior psychiatric admission

**Key: A and C.** Current thoughts establish acute concern; a specific plan with accessible lethal means increases immediacy. The next item in the case can ask students to select urgent evaluation, keeping the client in sight, and environmental safety actions. NIMH guidance includes urgent evaluation for current suicidal thoughts and discussion of securing/removing lethal means: <https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-conducted-at-nimh/asq-toolkit-materials/adult-outpatient/adult-outpatient-brief-suicide-safety-assessment-guide>.

### 3. Lithium safety

A client taking lithium reports three days of diarrhea after beginning a low-sodium diet. The nurse observes a coarse tremor and unsteady gait. Which action should the nurse take first?

A. Administer the scheduled lithium with a full glass of water.  
B. Hold the lithium and notify the prescriber for urgent evaluation and a serum level.  
C. Encourage the client to continue restricting sodium until the diarrhea resolves.  
D. Give an over-the-counter antidiarrheal and reassess tomorrow.

**Key: B.** Sodium depletion and dehydration increase lithium-toxicity risk. Current labeling emphasizes maintaining normal salt intake and hydration and monitoring for toxicity: <https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=803eab29-0d0a-4df0-b504-bcd85ec01ead&version=11>.

### 4. ECT indication

A client has severe major depressive disorder, active suicide risk, minimal fluid intake, and no meaningful response to two adequate medication trials. Which rationale best explains why the treatment team is considering electroconvulsive therapy?

A. ECT is the preferred first treatment for mild depressive symptoms.  
B. ECT can provide a more rapid response when severe depression is life-threatening.  
C. ECT permanently prevents future depressive episodes.  
D. ECT does not require anesthesia or physiologic monitoring.

**Key: B.** ECT may be considered for severe treatment-resistant depression or when rapid response is required because of suicidality, catatonia, or malnutrition: <https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/brain-stimulation-therapies/brain-stimulation-therapies>.

### 5. Lamotrigine rash

A client with bipolar I disorder began lamotrigine three weeks ago and now reports a new widespread rash. Which action should the nurse take?

A. Reassure the client that rash is expected during dose titration.  
B. Instruct the client to take the next dose with food.  
C. Hold the medication and arrange immediate prescriber evaluation.  
D. Recommend a topical antihistamine and reassess in one week.

**Key: C.** Lamotrigine labeling warns of potentially serious skin reactions and directs discontinuation at the first sign of rash unless the rash is clearly unrelated: <https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=d7e3572d-56fe-4727-2bb4-013ccca22678&type=display>.

### 6. Early antidepressant response

After two weeks of sertraline, a client reports improved sleep and appetite but continues to feel hopeless. Which interpretation and action are most appropriate?

A. The medication has failed; it should be stopped immediately.  
B. Early vegetative improvement can precede mood improvement; continue close monitoring, including direct suicide assessment.  
C. The client is developing mania because sleep has improved.  
D. The client no longer requires suicide-risk monitoring because energy is returning.

**Key: B.** Antidepressants commonly require 4–8 weeks for full benefit, and sleep, appetite, energy, or concentration may improve before mood. Close monitoring remains important early in treatment: <https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression>.

## Item-development workflow for July Quiz 3

1. Create the blueprint before writing stems; assign every item a content domain, nursing process/NCJMM function, client-needs category, response format, intended difficulty, and versioned item ID.
2. Build one three-item suicide mini-case that progresses from recognizing cues to assessing acuity to taking action. Do not copy the Exam 2 case.
3. Include at least one lithium item and one ECT item because these recur on Exam 2 and were absent from prior Quiz 3 forms.
4. Use alternate scenarios for the strongest historical concepts rather than exact-stem reuse.
5. Make distractors homogeneous and clinically plausible. Avoid one therapeutic response surrounded by three obviously punitive, reassuring, or “why” responses.
6. After administration, calculate local facility and corrected item-rest correlation. Review any item below .55 or above .90 facility, any local correlation below .10, and any unexpected response pattern.
7. Do not retire an otherwise sound item on one small cohort alone. Accumulate at least two administrations unless the key, rationale, rendering, or clinical content is visibly flawed.

## Reproducibility and limitations

The accompanying base-R script reproduces the aggregate, progression, reliability, item, topic-alignment, screening-cutoff, and Quiz 3-to-Exam 2 analyses. It runs against the included deidentified CSV extracts without installing packages. If `readxl` is installed, the current workbook can instead be supplied as a command-line argument.

Important limits:

- Cohort sizes are 9–19 students, so individual item correlations are noisy.
- Repeated stems are not independent item evidence.
- Alpha is affected by the multidimensional blueprint, partial-credit scoring, and the very narrow May score distribution.
- Correlation does not establish that Quiz 3 caused Exam 2 performance.
- The clinical recommendations should be checked against the course’s required textbook, local policy, and faculty-approved test blueprint before use.
