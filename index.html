Teaching

I teach clinical judgment as something students develop through explanation, practice, feedback, and revision. My aim is a classroom where students can ask questions, make their reasoning visible, and learn from uncertainty.

Case discussions connect assessment findings to decisions about care. Students practice explaining what they notice, considering what else they need to know, and revising their reasoning as new information becomes available.

At Chamberlain University, I serve as course lead for Psychiatric & Mental Health Nursing and teach in Adult Health and the Health Assessment lab. My earlier teaching at Cornell spanned psychology, human development, and leadership.

About

I am an assistant professor of nursing at Chamberlain University and a writer based in Atlanta. My scholarship brings nursing into conversation with psychology, ethics, and the health humanities.

I studied psychology at Georgia Tech and developmental psychology at Cornell, where my research focused on autobiographical memory. I earned my nursing degree at Emory and a master’s in nursing education at Western Governors University.

My clinical background includes emergency nursing, intensive care in Brooklyn during the first wave of COVID-19, and psychiatric nursing leadership. These experiences continue to shape my questions about the responsibilities of clinicians, educators, and institutions.

My essays, poetry, and fiction explore care, suffering, memory, and the stories people tell about their lives. Across scholarship and creative writing, I return to what it means to attend closely to another person, including when an answer or a remedy is uncertain.

I live with my wife and two dogs in Atlanta. My longstanding Buddhist practice also shapes my attention to language and everyday life.
