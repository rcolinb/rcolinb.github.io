Teaching

Clinical judgment, psychological safety & instructional design

I teach nursing as an assistant professor in Atlanta. The classroom is where my scholarship earns its keep: what I write about recognition, psychological safety, and clinical judgment is what I try to practice with students.

Current Teaching

  • Psychiatric-Mental Health Nursing
  • Adult Health
  • Health Assessment

Prior Teaching

Before nursing, I taught at Cornell in human development, cultural psychology, cognitive neuroscience, leadership, and community organization.

Teaching Philosophy

  • Clinical judgment as a developmental capacity
  • Application, comparison, and decision-making rather than fact accumulation
  • Psychological safety and accountability as mutually dependent
  • Systems thinking and attention to how educational environments shape behavior

Areas of Teaching Interest

  • Psychiatric and behavioral health nursing
  • Clinical judgment and nursing decision-making
  • Therapeutic communication
  • Trauma-informed clinical education
  • Simulation and unfolding cases
  • Assessment design and NCLEX preparation
  • Developmental and cognitive science in nursing education
  • Systems thinking and quality improvement

Instructional Design & Simulation

I build interactive teaching tools. The most developed is a browser-based cardiac physiology simulator that lets students manipulate the cardiac cycle and watch the ECG, pressures, and volumes respond in real time: the design problem was making the relationship between electrical and mechanical events something students could experiment with rather than memorize.

I also design unfolding case studies and simulation experiences that ask students to make sequential clinical decisions as a situation evolves, with structured debriefing built around clinical judgment rather than right answers.

Proprietary course materials, exam items, and student or institutional content are not published here. Where a tool or design cannot be shared, I am glad to discuss the method and educational purpose directly.
